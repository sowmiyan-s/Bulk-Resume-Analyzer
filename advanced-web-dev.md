# Design Thinking

Before starting to code, think about the user's business or company.

**Purpose**: Think about the purpose of the website.

**Tone**: Consider how the design should look based on the type of business, such as e-commerce, sales, online services, portfolio, private website, influencer website, software website, help center website, etc.

**Requirements**: Think about the framework, performance, and optimization required for the website.

**CRITICAL**: Choose a clear conceptual direction and execute it with precision. Bold maximalism and refined minimalism both work—the key is intentionality, not intensity.

## Frontend Aesthetics Guidelines

**Typography**: Avoid using traditional designs and fonts that are commonly used on every website. Use clear fonts, preferably unique or less commonly used fonts that match the brand. Also, research fonts used by popular similar websites and select ones that fit the project.

**Color**: The color scheme must match the brand identity if provided, such as colors used in the logo and branding. Avoid traditional patterns commonly selected by AI. Create a clean color palette chosen by humans based on color theory and human psychology principles.

**Motion**: Add animations, scroll effects, and hover effects only when required for the type of business website. Do not overload the website with animations. They should be smooth and flexible.

**Structure**: Create the best structure based on reference websites and avoid making it look AI-generated. Avoid generic AI patterns such as unnecessary header icons, "Built with Love" text, creator names in the footer, or dummy data. Make it realistic and deployment-ready. **Assets**: Use images, PNGs, SVGs, and other resources from free, non-copyrighted sources that match the theme and project requirements.

**Background & Visuals**: Avoid AI-style background designs. Instead, use traditional developer-preferred designs such as clean white backgrounds, patterns from uiverse.io, and visuals that match the business type.

NEVER use generic AI-generated aesthetics such as overused font families (Inter, Roboto, Arial, or system fonts), cliché color schemes (especially purple gradients on white backgrounds), predictable layouts, repetitive component patterns, or cookie-cutter designs that lack context-specific character.

Interpret creatively and make unexpected choices that feel genuinely designed for the context. No design should be the same. Vary between light and dark themes, different fonts, and different aesthetics. NEVER converge on common choices (such as Space Grotesk) across different generations.

**IMPORTANT**: Match implementation complexity to the aesthetic vision. Maximalist designs require elaborate code with extensive animations and effects. Minimalist or refined designs require restraint, precision, and careful attention to spacing, typography, and subtle details. Elegance comes from executing the vision well.

Remember: You are capable of extraordinary creative work. Don't hold back. Show what can truly be created by thinking outside the box and fully committing to a distinctive vision.
